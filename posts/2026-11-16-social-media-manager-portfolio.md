Every new social media manager meets the same wall. You pitch a business, and they reply, "Can I see your past work?" You have none, because nobody has hired you yet. And nobody will hire you until you have some.

The good news: a social media manager portfolio does not need to be full of paid clients. It needs to show that you can do the job. In this guide, we will show you exactly what to put in it, three ways to create real work from scratch, a simple case study template, where to host it for free, and how to share it when you pitch.

{{image:social-media-manager-portfolio-building-illustration}}

## What is a social media manager portfolio?

A social media manager portfolio is a short collection of your work that proves you can grow and manage a brand's social media. Think of it as your shop window. A CV says what you claim you can do. A portfolio shows it.

When a business owner in Lagos or Abuja looks at your portfolio, they are quietly asking three questions:

1. **Do you understand businesses like mine?**
2. **Can you make content that looks good and sounds right?**
3. **Will it bring me results, such as more DMs, orders or bookings?**

Everything in your portfolio should answer one of those questions. Pretty designs alone do not answer the third one. That is why case studies matter more than a gallery of flyers.

## What to put in your social media manager portfolio

Keep your portfolio short and focused. A busy client should understand it in two or three minutes on their phone. Here is what to include:

- **A short intro.** One or two lines: who you help and how. For example, "I help food and beauty businesses in Lagos turn Instagram followers into WhatsApp orders."
- **Your services.** Content planning, design, Reels, captions, community management, simple ads, monthly reports. Only list what you can actually deliver.
- **Two or three case studies.** This is the heart of your portfolio. More on this below.
- **Content samples.** Your best posts, carousels, Reels and captions, ideally inside each case study.
- **Results.** Real numbers, with screenshots from Instagram insights or Meta Business Suite.
- **Your tools.** For example Canva, CapCut and Meta Business Suite.
- **Testimonials,** but only real ones. If you do not have any yet, leave this section out.
- **A clear way to reach you.** WhatsApp link, email or a booking link.

## How to build a social media manager portfolio with no experience

"No experience" usually means "no paid clients". You can still create real work. Here are three ways, and you should try to do all three.

### 1. Run your own page as a live project

Start or restart an Instagram or TikTok page in the niche you want to work in. If you want food clients, run a page about Lagos food spots or easy Nigerian recipes. If you want beauty clients, post skincare tips or makeup looks.

Treat it like a client account. Set a goal, plan your content, post consistently for at least 30 days and track your numbers every week. Your own page is the one project nobody can stop you from doing today.

### 2. Manage a real business for free (for a set time)

Find one small business that needs help: a friend's fashion brand, your aunty's catering business, your church, a student association, or a shop on your street. Offer to manage their page for free for four to six weeks.

Agree on three things in writing first, even in a WhatsApp chat:

- What you will do (for example, three posts and two Reels a week).
- How long it will run.
- That you can show the work and results in your portfolio.

Free work should have an end date. After it ends, you either move to a paid package or you part as friends with a strong case study.

### 3. Create one concept project

A concept project (also called "spec work") is work you create for a real brand that did not hire you, to show what you would do. For example, pick a Lagos restaurant with a messy Instagram page and create a one-month content plan, a new grid of nine posts and a sample Reel script.

{{image:spec-work-concept-project-illustration}}

Be honest about it. Label it clearly as a **concept project**, not client work, and never suggest the brand worked with you. Concept projects are great for showing how you think, but pair them with at least one live project that has real results.

## How to write a social media case study

A case study is a short story of one project: where it started, what you did and what happened. This is what turns "I can design posts" into "I can get you results".

Use the same structure for every project, so clients can scan them quickly:

{{image:social-media-case-study-template-table}}

A few tips to make your case studies stronger:

- **Start with the goal, not the graphics.** "Help a Surulere cake business get more orders from Instagram" is more interesting than "I designed 12 posts".
- **Explain your thinking.** Why did you choose those content pillars? Why Reels on Tuesdays? Clients pay for judgement, not just Canva skills.
- **Keep it short.** Three or four short paragraphs, a handful of visuals and a results section is enough.
- **Show what you learned.** "Next time I would test more behind-the-scenes Reels" shows maturity.

## How to show results when you are just starting

{{image:social-media-case-study-before-and-after-illustration}}

Results do not have to be huge. They have to be real. A small business page that went from two DMs a week to ten is a good result. A page that grew from 300 to 900 followers in a month, with screenshots to prove it, is a good result.

Useful things to measure:

- **Reach** and how many of those people were not following yet.
- **Shares and saves**, which show content people found useful.
- **Profile visits and link taps.**
- **DMs and WhatsApp chats started.** For many Nigerian businesses, this is the number that leads to sales.
- **Orders or bookings**, if the owner can tell you.

Always take a "before" screenshot on day one, then an "after" screenshot at the end. **Never invent or round up numbers, never buy followers, and never use fake testimonials.** Clients compare notes, and one fake claim can end your career before it starts.

## Where to host your portfolio for free

{{image:where-to-host-social-media-portfolio-illustration}}

You do not need to pay for a website on day one. You need one clean link that opens fast on a phone. Good free options:

- **A Canva website.** You can design a simple one-page site in Canva and publish it for free on a Canva address, as [Canva's help centre explains](https://www.canva.com/help/article/publishing-websites-for-free/). It is easy if you already design in Canva.
- **A Notion page.** Clean, quick to update, and good for written case studies.
- **A PDF on Google Drive.** Design it in Canva, export it, upload it and set sharing to "anyone with the link". Keep the file small so it opens on mobile data.
- **Behance.** Useful if your work is very visual, such as design-heavy carousels and brand grids.

Also use your own Instagram. Add your portfolio link to your bio and create a Story Highlight called "Portfolio" or "My work". Many clients will check your page before they open any link.

Whatever you choose, test the link on your own phone, on mobile data, before you send it to anyone.

## Social media portfolio mistakes to avoid

- **Showing only designs.** Flyers without goals or results look like a graphic design portfolio, not social media management.
- **Too much work.** Thirty random posts hide your best ones. Pick your strongest.
- **Fake or "borrowed" work.** Using another person's designs or a big brand's real campaign as yours is a quick way to lose trust.
- **No niche.** A food client wants to see food work. Lead with projects that match the client you are pitching.
- **Hard-to-open links.** Huge files, broken links or pages that need a login.
- **Never updating it.** Replace your weakest project every time you finish a better one.
