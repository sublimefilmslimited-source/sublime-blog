Should You Hire a Social Media Manager or an Agency? Pros, Cons and Costs

One person or a whole team? Here is how to decide who should run your social media, what each option really costs you, and how to stay in control either way.

Sooner or later, every growing business reaches the same point. You are tired of posting at midnight, replying to DMs between customers and wondering why your page is not bringing sales. So you decide to get help. Then the real question comes: should you hire a social media manager or an agency?

Both can work very well. Both can also waste your money if you choose for the wrong reasons. In this guide, we compare the two honestly: the pros and cons of each, how the costs differ, who each option suits, the questions to ask before you pay anyone, and how to keep control of your accounts.

We run an agency and we also train social media managers at our school, so we see both sides every week.

What is the difference between a social media manager and an agency?

A social media manager is one person who plans, creates and posts content for your business, replies to comments and messages, and reports on results. They can work in two ways:

  • Freelance: they work for several businesses at once and you pay a monthly fee.
  • In-house: they are your staff. They work only for you and you pay a salary.

A social media agency is a company with a team. Different people handle strategy, design, video, copywriting, ads and reporting. You usually deal with one main contact, often called an account manager, who coordinates the rest of the team.

The simplest way to think about it: with a manager, you hire a person. With an agency, you hire a team and a process.

Pros and cons of hiring a social media manager

The pros

  • Lower cost. You are paying for one person’s time, so the monthly fee is usually lower than an agency’s.
  • Direct contact. You talk to the person doing the work. No middle person.
  • Close to your business. A good manager learns your products, your customers and your tone quickly. An in-house manager can walk into your shop in Ikeja and film new stock the same day it arrives.
  • Flexible. It is easy to start small, for example with Instagram only, and add more later.

The cons

  • One person, limited skills. Very few people are equally strong at strategy, design, video editing, copywriting and ads. Something usually gets less attention.
  • No cover. If they fall sick, travel, write exams or take on too many clients, your page can go quiet.
  • You must manage them. Someone in your business needs to approve content, give feedback and check results.
  • Hard to judge at first. Anyone can call themselves a social media manager. You need to check their work carefully.

Pros and cons of hiring a social media agency

The pros

  • A full team. Strategy, design, video, ads and reporting are handled by people who focus on each skill.
  • Steady cover. If one person is away, someone else picks up the work, so your page keeps running.
  • Process and reporting. A good agency has a clear way of planning, approving and reporting, so you spend less time chasing.
  • Stronger with paid ads. Agencies that run ads for many businesses usually have more experience testing and improving campaigns.
  • Experience across industries. They have seen what works for restaurants, real estate, fashion and events, and can bring those lessons to you.

The cons

  • Higher cost. You are paying for a team, office and process.
  • Less personal. You may not speak with the designer or video editor directly.
  • You may be a small client. Some agencies give their best people to their biggest accounts. Ask who exactly will handle your page.
  • Slower for small changes. A quick post about today’s promo may need to pass through more hands.

How much does each option cost in Nigeria?

There is no single price for either option. Quotes depend on how many platforms you want, how many posts and videos each month, whether someone replies to your DMs all day, and whether ads are included.

What matters most is how you compare quotes. Every quote has three parts:

  1. The management fee: what you pay for the work and skill.
  2. Content production: photo or video shoots, models, props and editing. Sometimes included, sometimes extra.
  3. Ad spend: the money paid to Meta or TikTok to show your ads. This is almost always separate from the fee.

A freelancer’s quote will usually be lower than an agency’s. But a low quote that leaves out video, ads and reporting can end up costing you more than a higher quote that covers everything you need.

If you are thinking about a full-time, in-house manager, count the full cost, not just the salary: their phone, data, laptop, any paid tools, pension and other staff costs, and the time you or your manager will spend supervising them.

Which one is right for your business?

Use these simple rules of thumb.

A social media manager is often the better fit if:

  • You are a small or new business, for example a home bakery, a boutique or a solo consultant.
  • You need one or two platforms, usually Instagram plus WhatsApp or TikTok.
  • Your content is simple: product photos, short videos, captions and replies.
  • You have time to give feedback and check the work every week.

An agency is often the better fit if:

  • Social media already brings in a large share of your sales, so mistakes are costly.
  • You need regular video, good design and paid ads working together.
  • You are launching something big: a new branch, a product, an event or a mall opening.
  • You want one team to be responsible for results, with a clear monthly report.

A mix can also work. Some businesses keep an in-house person for daily posts and DMs, and use an agency for strategy, shoots and ads. This is common once a business grows.

Still not sure? Walk through these five steps.

The last step matters most. Whoever you hire, agree a three-month trial with clear targets. One month is too short to judge. Three months is enough to see if things are moving.

Questions to ask before you hire a social media manager or agency

Before you pay anyone, ask these questions and get the answers in writing.

A few things to listen for in the answers:

  • Real results, not just pretty designs. Ask to see before-and-after numbers for a business like yours: more DMs, enquiries, bookings or sales, not only likes.
  • Clear names. “Our team will handle it” is not an answer. Ask who will design, who will write and who will reply to your customers.
  • Honest promises. Be careful with anyone who guarantees a fixed number of followers or sales. Nobody controls the algorithm, and some “guaranteed growth” comes from bought followers that never buy anything.
  • A clear exit. You should be able to end the contract with fair notice and keep all your accounts, content and passwords.

How to stay in control of your social media accounts

This is the part many business owners skip, and it causes the most pain later. If a manager leaves on bad terms and your business does not own the accounts, getting your page back can be very hard.

Follow these rules whichever option you choose:

  • Your business must own every account. That means your Facebook Page, Instagram account, TikTok account and ad accounts. Use an email address and phone number your business controls.
  • Do not share passwords. Meta lets a business >give a partner access to business assets in your business portfolio, such as your Page or ad account. The agency or manager gets the access they need, and you can remove it later.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (a code sent to your phone or an app each time someone logs in) on every account.
  • Keep your own copies. Ask for your designs, videos and content calendar to be saved in a shared folder you own, such as Google Drive.
  • Pay ad spend from your own card or account where possible, so you always see what was spent.

How to get the best from whoever you hire

A good start makes everything easier. In your first meeting, agree on:

  • One main goal for the first three months, for example more WhatsApp orders or more bookings.
  • Your brand basics: logo, colours, fonts, tone of voice, prices, delivery areas and the questions customers ask most.
  • Approval rules: what needs your OK before posting, and how fast you will reply.
  • A monthly report and call: what was done, what worked and what changes next month.
  • Who replies to DMs, and when. For many Nigerian businesses the sale happens in the DM, so this matters a lot.

Then give them time and honest feedback. Even the best manager or agency needs your input to sound like your business. If you want to understand what good Instagram work looks like before you hire, our Instagram marketing playbook for small businesses is a good place to start.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to hire a social media manager or an agency?

Neither is better for everyone. A social media manager suits a small business with one or two platforms, simple content and a tight budget. An agency suits a business that needs strategy, video, design and paid ads together, or one where social media brings in a big share of sales. Start from your goal and the amount of work, then compare quotes.

Is a social media agency more expensive than a freelancer?

Usually, yes, because you are paying for a team rather than one person. But compare what each quote includes before you decide. A cheaper freelancer quote that leaves out video, ads and reporting can cost more in the end than an agency package that covers everything. Always ask for the management fee, content costs and ad spend to be shown separately.

Should a small business hire a full-time social media manager?

Only when there is enough work to keep one person busy every day, such as several platforms, daily posting, lots of DMs and regular video. A full-time hire also means a salary every month, equipment, data and time spent managing them. Many small businesses start with a freelancer or an agency and hire in-house once social media is clearly bringing in sales.

What should I check before giving someone access to my Instagram or Facebook page?

Never share your password. Keep your business as the owner of the Facebook Page, Instagram account and ad account, then give the manager or agency access through Meta's business tools. Turn on two-factor authentication, agree in writing what they can and cannot do, and make sure you can remove their access at any time if you stop working together.

How long before I see results from a social media manager or agency?

Give any new manager or agency about three months. The first month is usually spent learning your business, fixing your profile and testing content. By the end of the third month, you should see clear movement in the goal you agreed, such as more DMs, enquiries or orders. Ask for a monthly report so you can judge progress along the way.
